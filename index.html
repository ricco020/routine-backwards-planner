Routine builder: start times worked back from when you must be done

Most routines fail at the start, not the end: "I need to leave at 8:15" does not say when the shower has to begin. List the steps with the minutes you think each one takes, enter the time you must be done, and the planner gives the clock time to start every step, working backwards. A buffer pads each step because guesses tend to run short. Log how long a few steps really took and it shows the buffer your own numbers call for, instead of a rule of thumb.

Steps, in order (your guess in minutes, and "really took" after a timed run if you have it)

How the start times are worked out

Each step gets its guess plus the buffer, rounded up to a whole minute (a 7-minute step with 25 % becomes 9 minutes). The last step ends at the "done by" time, the step before it ends where the last one starts, and so on up to the first step, whose start is the time to begin. If the routine is long enough to start before midnight, the start is marked "the day before".

Where the suggested buffer comes from

For the steps where you filled in "really took", the planner adds up the real minutes and the guessed minutes. If the real total is 1.3 times the guess, the suggested buffer is 30 %. It only uses your own timed runs, so it means more after a few days than after one. When your guesses are longer than reality, it suggests 0 %.

Limits

A buffer per step assumes the overrun is spread across steps. If one step always runs late (the step with a screen in it), give that step a longer guess instead. This is a planning tool, not a treatment or a medical tool. Nothing is sent anywhere: the routine is stored in this browser only.
